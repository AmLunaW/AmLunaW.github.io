I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Systems and Industrial Engineering at the University of Arizona. My research develops optimization and data-driven decision-support methods for operational decisions made under uncertainty, when demand is unpredictable, time is critical, resources are limited, and disruptions such as wildfires and extreme weather can change conditions quickly. I apply these methods to problems that touch daily life and carry direct social impact, spanning:

  • Logistics and supply chain management: placing inventory to meet fast-fulfillment demand, planning delivery routes in time-sensitive environments, designing fleets across shipping options, and enabling cooperative procurement and distribution for small and medium-sized enterprises.
  • Sustainable food and agricultural systems: allocating and packaging rescued food under uncertain demand, managing perishable inventory and donations, scheduling food redistribution fairly under limited capacity, and optimizing irrigation in arid regions.
  • Resilient and safe transportation infrastructure: guiding post-wildfire highway maintenance and vegetation recovery, and evaluating the impact of driver-safety programs on crash risk.

Prior to joining the University of Arizona, I received my Ph.D. in Industrial Engineering from the Wm Michael Barnes ‘64 Department of Industrial and Systems Engineering at Texas A&M University under the supervision of Prof. Joseph Geunes and Prof. Xiaofeng Nie. I also hold both an M.S. and a B.S. in Transportation from Southeast University, China.
